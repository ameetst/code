MOMENTUM INVESTING LONG TERM (MILT) STRATEGY
Summary of Rakesh Pujara's CWA MILT 25 Framework
(From Sanjay Kathuria Podcast EP70)
================================================================================

OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The CWA MILT 25 (Momentum Investing Long Term) strategy is a strictly rule-based, 
algorithm-driven system designed to eliminate human emotion, biases, and the 
need for fundamental forecasting. It operates on the core philosophy of momentum: 
"What is in motion tends to stay in motion." 

By ruthlessly cutting losers and letting winners run unchecked, the strategy 
aims to capture massive "alpha" from a few multi-bagger stocks over a long period. 
Historically, this specific approach yielded approximately 27% CAGR over 3 years.

1. UNIVERSE & SIZING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Target Universe: Top 750 stocks on the National Stock Exchange (NSE). This 
  includes Large, Mid, Small, and Micro-caps. Limiting to the index ensures a 
  baseline of fundamentals and liquidity (generally companies above ~2000 Cr 
  market cap), avoiding highly manipulated or illiquid stocks.
* Portfolio Limit: Maximum 25 stocks at any given time.
* Allocation: Equal weight initial allocation of 4% per stock.

2. ENTRY RULES (THE SETUP)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Timeframe: Weekly charts. Scans are run after the Friday market close.
* Core Indicator: Bollinger Bands.
* The Setup: Instead of the standard 2.0 Standard Deviations (SD), this strategy 
  uses an extreme 3.7 Standard Deviations on the upper band.
* The Logic: 95% of price action falls within 2.0 SD. By requiring a close above 
  3.7 SD, the strategy ignores "normal" stocks and only enters the top 1% of 
  extreme outliers exhibiting explosive, unquestionable momentum.
* Execution: If a stock closes above the 3.7 SD band on Friday, buy at the open 
  on Monday morning.
* Tie-Breakers: If the market is highly bullish and more than 25 stocks trigger 
  a buy signal, filter the top 25 using Relative Strength (RS) or 12-month 
  Rate of Change (ROC).

3. EXIT RULES (THE SAFEGUARDS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Once invested, a stock is sold if it hits ANY of the following three conditions 
based on a Friday close:

1. Initial Stop Loss: A 20% drop from the purchase price. This protects against 
   false breakouts.
2. Trend Reversal (Trailing Stop): A weekly close below the 23-Week Moving Average. 
   This helps capture the bulk of the profit once a stock has run up significantly, 
   ensuring you don't ride a winner all the way back down.
3. Vertical Fall Protection (ATR): A weekly close below the Average True Range 
   (ATR), using settings of 14 periods and a 1.8 multiplier. 
   Why use this? Sometimes a stock moves up vertically (e.g., jumps 4x in a few 
   months). In such cases, the 23-week MA will be too far below the current price. 
   If a sudden crash occurs, the ATR acts as a tighter circuit breaker to lock 
   in gains before the stock drops all the way back to the moving average.

4. PORTFOLIO MAINTENANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Do NOT Rebalance Winners: If a stock grows from a 4% allocation to a 10% or 
  15% allocation due to price appreciation, leave it alone. Selling winners to 
  rebalance is punishing merit. The outsized returns ("alpha") come from these 
  runaway winners.
* First-In, First-Out (FIFO): Stick with the stocks that triggered first in the 
  momentum cycle. Late-stage signals in a bull cycle are often weaker stocks.
* Low Time Commitment: The system demands zero attention from Tuesday to Thursday. 
  Check signals on the weekend, execute on Monday morning (takes ~15 minutes a week).
* Dealing with Drawdowns: During massive market corrections where momentum dies, 
  the portfolio will naturally trigger stop-losses and transition into cash/liquid 
  funds. For peace of mind, investors can set rules to park cash in Gold or T-Bills 
  until momentum returns.

CONCLUSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The MILT strategy forces an investor to do what is psychologically hardest: 
buy stocks that have already gone up significantly, hold onto massive profits 
without selling early, and accept small losses quickly without hoping they bounce 
back.
